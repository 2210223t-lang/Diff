\section{Introduction}

Cosmology concerns the origin, structure, and evolution of
the Universe. Its subject includes space, time, matter,
radiation, and several questions which would have been
considerably easier had the Universe supplied documentation.

The scale of the problem presents certain practical difficulties.
The Universe cannot conveniently be placed on a laboratory bench,
restarted under controlled conditions, or replaced during
the warranty period. We must therefore rely on observations,
mathematical models, and a sufficiently confident presentation.

This work develops the last of these methods in particular.

\subsection{Preliminary cosmological considerations}

On large scales, cosmology describes an expanding Universe
in which gravity influences the evolution of matter and
the formation of structure. A detailed treatment involves
general relativity and the geometry of spacetime.

A complete introduction to these subjects would require
a substantial volume. Fortunately, the present work is
also a substantial volume, although for different reasons.

For our purposes, three observations will suffice:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The Universe changes.
    \item Mathematics provides a language for describing change.
    \item Differentiation is therefore likely to appear
          sooner or later.
\end{enumerate}

The reader may feel that the third observation follows
rather quickly from the first two. This is an excellent
indication of readiness for the remainder of the book.

\subsection{The central cosmological function}

We now introduce the central object of our investigation:
the \emph{main cosmological function}.

Its selection follows a rigorous procedure. An expression
is supplied by the reader, accepted by the program, and
subsequently typeset in a manner appropriate to its new
scientific importance.

Let this function be denoted by
\[
    \mathcal{C}(x) = \CosmicFunction.
\]

Here, \(x\) denotes the independent variable. Its precise
physical interpretation will be addressed once it becomes
necessary to defend one.

The connection between this expression and the observable
Universe is left open. This preserves the generality of
the method and avoids restricting its applicability
to expressions with an actual cosmological interpretation.

\subsection{Method of investigation}

Our immediate objective is to determine
\[
    \frac{d\mathcal{C}}{dx}.
\]

Where differentiation is performed, we work on a domain
where the expression and the required derivatives exist.

The calculation will proceed through a sequence of
carefully selected steps. Some will be explained in detail.
Others will follow from standard results, elementary
considerations, or the great theorem of uniform comprehensibility%
\footnote{
    A transition that is obvious to the lecturer is considered
    clear to the entire audience, uniformly with respect
    to the audience's level of preparation.
    The proof is omitted as an immediate application
    of the theorem itself.
}.

The amount of explanation accompanying a step should not
be confused with its mathematical difficulty.
A particularly short explanation may indicate
a particularly ambitious omission.

Having established the necessary foundations, we now turn
to the calculation itself. The reader is encouraged
to keep a pencil nearby, since several responsibilities
are about to be transferred.
